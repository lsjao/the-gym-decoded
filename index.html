02Biology of Adaptation

Muscles grow by responding to mechanical tension.

The 3-Step Adaptation Loop

Mechanical Tension: Physical force and strain inside muscle fibers resisting a load triggers tissue repair and growth.

Nerdy Trick: Lengthened Stretch

Control the lowering phase into a comfortable stretch. Muscles respond strongly when loaded at longer lengths.

Peer-Reviewed
Schoenfeld, B. J. (2010): The mechanisms of muscle hypertrophy and their application to resistance training, J. Strength Cond. Res. • Wolf et al. & Kassiano et al. (2023): Systematic reviews showing strong hypertrophy when loading muscles at longer (stretched) lengths.
03Load, Sets & Trade-Offs

Heavy sets vs. lighter sets: choosing your trade-offs.

Strength + Size ~5 to 10 Reps

1–2 Hard Sets (Heavier Weight)

Builds maximal strength and muscle size efficiently.

Pros

Recruits high-threshold fibers from rep 1 and keeps workouts brief.

Cons

Higher central nervous system (CNS) fatigue and joint stress, requiring longer recovery.

Endurance + Stamina ~15 to 30+ Reps

3–5 Lighter Sets (Lighter Weight)

Builds local muscular endurance and stamina.

Pros

Gentler on joints and CNS while building muscular work capacity.

Cons

Takes longer; only builds comparable size if pushed right up to muscular failure.

04Exercise Selection

Six basic movement patterns cover your entire body.

Pro Tip: Start on machines & cables for built-in stability

1. Push

Pushing a heavy door open. Trains chest, front shoulders, and triceps.

2. Pull

Pulling a suitcase toward you. Trains upper back, lats, and biceps.

3. Squat

Sitting down and standing back up. Trains quads and glutes.

4. Hinge

Picking a box off the floor safely. Trains hamstrings and glutes.

5. Lunge

Climbing stairs one leg at a time. Trains balance and leg symmetry.

6. Carry / Brace

Carrying heavy groceries. Trains deep core and spine stability.

Pick 1 exercise per pattern for a balanced, full-body session. 05 / 10

08Evidence-Based Habits

Four nerdy rules that save beginners months of guesswork.

Rule 01 • Double Progression

Earn reps before adding weight

Keep the same weight until you hit 12 clean reps, then add 5 lbs and reset to 8 reps.

Rule 02 • Exercise Order

Compounds first, isolations second

Train multi-joint presses, rows, and leg presses while fresh before single-joint curls or extensions.

Rule 03 • Rest 2 to 3 Minutes

Full rest protects set quality

Taking 2 to 3 minutes between hard sets restores energy so your next set stays strong.

Rule 04 • Soreness Is Not Progress

DOMS measures novelty, not growth

Soreness reflects an unfamiliar stimulus, while steady rep progression confirms real adaptation.

Peer-Reviewed
Schoenfeld et al. (2016) JSCR; Nunes et al. (2020) Eur J Sport Sci; Nosaka et al. (2002) Scand J Med Sci Sports.
